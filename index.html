The vowel space

Vowels are mapped by where the tongue sits: left is the front of the mouth, right is the back; top is a closed jaw, bottom is open. In each pair, the left vowel has spread lips and the right one has rounded lips. English speakers tend to glide vowels (eɪ, oʊ). Spanish, Mandarin and Arabic vowels stay still.

Mandarin tones in IPA

IPA writes pitch with "tone letters" (Chao letters). Picture a vertical scale from 1 (lowest in your voice) to 5 (highest). The line traces where your pitch starts and ends. ˥ is level and high (55), ˧˥ rises from mid to high (35), and so on. The same syllable with a different tone is a different word.

Tips for English speakers

  • Tone 1 is higher and flatter than you think. Hold it like you're singing one note.
  • Tone 2 sounds like a surprised "huh?" or "what?".
  • Tone 3 is mostly low. In normal speech it often stays low (˨˩) and only rises at the end of a phrase. Before another tone 3 it changes to tone 2: 你好 nǐ hǎo is said ní hǎo.
  • Tone 4 is a sharp fall, like a firm "No!" or "Stop!"
  • The neutral tone (轻声) is short and light, with pitch that follows the syllable before it, as in 妈妈 māma.

Read Arabic

IPA in five minutes

The IPA gives one symbol to each sound, in any language. English spelling can't do that ("ough" is pronounced five ways), but [x] always means the same sound, whether it's spelled j in Spanish, h in pinyin or خ in Arabic.

Brackets: [square] brackets show how something is actually pronounced. /slashes/ show the "mental" sound category. Spanish dedo is /dedo/ but is pronounced [ˈd̪eðo].

Reading a consonant's description

Every consonant is described with three words, e.g. voiced velar fricative:

  • Voicing: voiced means your vocal cords buzz (put fingers on your throat for "zzz" vs "sss").
  • Place: where the blockage is, from the lips (bilabial) to the voice box (glottal). The coloured strip on each card shows the place.
  • Manner: how the air gets through. A stop fully blocks it, a fricative squeezes it into a hiss, a trill vibrates, a tap touches once, and an approximant barely narrows the passage.

Practise the jargon

Words like velar and fricative stick faster when you use them. Tap the right spot in the mouth, or match a term to its meaning. The terms you miss come back more often.

Marks you'll see

pʰʰ aspiration
A puff of air after the sound. English "pin" has it, "spin" doesn't. Mandarin p/t/k/c/ch/q have it.
sˤˤ pharyngealised
The back of the tongue pulls toward the throat. These are Arabic's "emphatic" letters.
aːː long
Hold the sound longer. In Arabic, length changes meaning: katab "he wrote" vs kaːteb "writer".
t̪̪ dental
Tongue touches the back of the teeth, not the gum ridge. Spanish t and d work this way.
ˈperoˈ stress
Placed before the stressed syllable.
